## Context and problem

A construction company needed to know at all times how much material was in its warehouse, what was going out to the site and what was coming in from suppliers.

## My role

The whole project, end to end: data model, views, interface and setup. It was the first system I built on my own.

## Solution

- **Inventory** with the stock and unit of each material (bags, meters, units).
- **Stock in and out** from the same table: each material has a field to consume and another to add stock.
- **Movement history** with the date, quantity, operation type and the stock left after each one.
- **Admin panel** using Django's admin with the Jazzmin theme, to manage materials and users.

## Architecture

- **Backend:** Django with two models, the material and its movement, and views that update the stock and record every operation.
- **Interface:** Django templates with Tailwind CSS 4.
- **Data:** SQLite.

## Results

It worked well locally and covered almost all of the warehouse management. Staff attendance tracking was left pending.

## What I'd do differently

Looking at it today, there are things I'd change, and I want to pick it up again to finish it:

- Update the stock inside a transaction, so two simultaneous operations can't throw it off.
- Validate quantities with a form instead of reading them by hand, and not disable CSRF protection.
- Move the secret key and debug mode to environment variables.
- Add automated tests and attendance tracking.
